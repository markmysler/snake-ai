**Question:** The baseline only sees one square around its head. Does looking further along 8 lines help it avoid traps?

**Change:** Vision: rays that look 4 squares, or 9 squares (across the whole board), along 8 directions instead of the 11 short hints.

**Your prediction:** Will 9 squares beat 4? Which method gains more from the extra inputs (28 instead of 11)? _Write yours down before you look at the results._

## Background

Rays report how close the nearest wall, body part and apple are on each of 8 lines. More range means more warning about its own body, but also more inputs to learn from.

**What to watch:** Use Inspect on a board to see the 8 rays and what each one found. Compare the test scores with lesson 01's baseline.

**Recipes**

- **rays 4**: vision rays:depth=4, reward score, Genetic algorithm + Deep Q-learning, 10 min × 3 seeds 0–2. Changes from “baseline” of lesson 01: vision short → rays:depth=4.
- **rays 9**: vision rays:depth=9, reward score, Genetic algorithm + Deep Q-learning, 10 min × 3 seeds 0–2. Changes from “baseline” of lesson 01: vision short → rays:depth=9.

## AI analysis

### Run `2026-10-20_131708-6` · rays 9

**Seeing further did not clearly help the GA: rays 9 averaged 29.3 test points over 3 seeds against about 34.7 for rays 4 (2 seeds shown), and the DQN was flat at 26.8 against about 26.5. Seed 2's GA starved in 36 of 100 test games.**

**Genetic algorithm (GA, evolving a population of networks without gradients):** Across seeds 0, 1 and 2 the GA's greedy test means were 32.07, 32.03 and 23.93 (mean 29.34, std 4.69). Seeds 0 and 1 mostly crashed (wall and body), with 0 and 5 starved games. Seed 2 starved in 36 games, took 1,038 steps per game and needed 43 steps per apple, so it wandered until it ran out of time. Its learning curve rose from about 0 to 17 by 0.8 min and to 30.2 by 10 min, still climbing slowly at the end. It ran about 68 million environment steps (about 113k steps per second).

**Deep Q-network (DQN, a gradient-trained value learner):** Test means were 27.38, 27.28 and 25.60 (mean 26.75, std 1.0). Its crashes were mostly into its own body (69 of 100 in seed 2), with 9 steps per apple, so it dies quickly rather than wandering. Its training mean plateaued near 19 to 20 after about 2 minutes, while greedy tests scored about 26. That gap is partly exploration, since training games include random moves. It ran about 1.1 million steps (about 2 thousand steps per second).

**Comparison notes:** Only two rays-4 seeds appear in this run's data (seeds 1 and 2: GA 33.23 and 36.07, DQN 27.89 and 25.12). The GA difference (about 5 points) is within roughly one standard deviation of the seed spread, so treat it as a lean, not a result. The DQN result is flat. The baseline (short vision, one seed) scored DQN 21.31. That suggests rays helped the DQN by about 5 points, but the baseline is a single seed and the GA baseline used a different reward (ga_classic), so its GA score of 51.55 cannot be compared with these runs.

**Why:** The GA's weakest seed fits a reward problem. The score reward gives 0 per move, -10 for a crash, and nothing for starving. A snake that loops in circles therefore scores better than one that dies, so selection can favour loopers. Seed 2 shows this, with 36 starved games and 43 steps per apple. Seeds 0 and 1, which crash instead, do better. The DQN has the opposite pattern: it learns quickly (14.75 training mean after under 1 minute, from about 111k steps) and then plateaus, and its crashes are fast body collisions. Nine-square rays give the DQN more to see, which plausibly explains the gain over the short baseline. For the GA, the extra inputs add weights to evolve, which may slow search within 10 minutes. With 3 seeds per group, these are hypotheses, not conclusions.

**What we learned:** Judge a change by its seed spread, not one run, and check how each game ended: a similar test score can hide a crashing snake and a wandering one. Also, the GA and DQN receive the same wall-clock time but very different numbers of steps, so compare them per step as well as by score.

**Try next:**
1. **Add a starvation penalty to the score reward** (reward: + starve 10). Starving currently costs nothing, so looping can beat dying for the GA. Adding a starve penalty should cut seed 2's 36 starved games and lift its GA score toward the other seeds (about 32). _Prediction:_ GA starved share falls from 36% to under 10% of test games, and the GA 3-seed mean rises from 29.3 to about 32 or more. DQN changes by less than 3 points.
2. **Train twice as long to see if the GA is still improving** (training time (min) 10 → 20). The GA was still rising at the end (28.75 at 7.6 min, 30.24 at 10 min), so 10 minutes may understate it. A longer run tests whether the rays-9 deficit is a time limit. _Prediction:_ GA test mean rises to 31 to 34 (from 23.9 for this seed). DQN plateaus near 26 and changes by less than 2 points.
3. **Try a picture of the board instead of rays** (vision rays:depth=9 → window:radius=2). Rays give 28 inputs but only along 8 lines. A 5x5 picture around the head (79 inputs) shows the squares near the body directly, which may help the DQN avoid body collisions, its main cause of death. _Prediction:_ DQN test mean stays within about 2 points of 26.8, and its body-collision share falls below 60% of test games. The GA changes less than the DQN.

### Run `2026-10-20_131708` · rays 4

**Rays depth 4 lifts DQN's test score to 26.7 from 21.3 for the short-vision baseline, but depth 9 adds nothing, and the GA's score is held back by looping: 34 of its 100 test games ended by starving.**

**GA (neuroevolution, a population of networks that breed and mutate):** test mean 28.62 on seed 0, with median 28 and best game 44. Across the three seeds it averages 32.64 (std 3.76; values 28.62, 33.23, 36.07). Its training curve climbs quickly, from 12.2 at 0.44 min to 30.0 at 3.9 min, then flattens near 30-31 for the last six minutes. That is the elite line the GA keeps. The full generation's mean (population_mean) rises from 4.2 to about 27, so the children are still improving. In 10 minutes it ran 79.6M env steps at about 130k steps per second.

**DQN (Deep Q-learning, which learns the value of each move from stored experience):** test mean 26.69 on seed 0 and 26.57 across seeds (std 1.39). Its training mean sits at 19-21 after the first minute. That is lower than the test score because exploration moves are included. It ran only 1.2M env steps at about 2k steps per second, so it is roughly 66 times slower per step than the GA.

**How the games ended:** the GA's seed-0 games took 1,372 steps and 48 steps per apple, and 34 games ended by starving (looping without eating). Its other seeds starved far less (11 and 3 games). DQN games are short, at 237 steps and 9 steps per apple, and end by crashing, about half into walls and half into its own body. It never starves.

**Why:** The depth change itself is clear. Compared with the short-vision baseline, DQN gained about 5 points (21.31 to 26.69) under the same reward and settings. That baseline is only one seed, though, and the three depth-4 seeds spread by 1.4, so this is a probable gain, not a settled one. Going from depth 4 to depth 9 changed nothing: GA 32.64 vs 29.34 and DQN 26.57 vs 26.75, with overlapping spreads. Most of the GA's apparent gap is its reward. The baseline GA used a survival bonus (0.1 per move) and an apple reward of 100, so its 51.55 is not comparable with this run.

The GA's starving is a reward problem. Its reward is only apples and deaths, so a snake that loops forever without dying pays nothing for wasting time. Starving is not penalised, and some seeds drift into loops. DQN's crashes are a different problem. With gamma 0.95 it values only about the next 20 moves, which is probably too short to see a body trap coming. Its speed (about 2k steps per second) also limits how much it can learn in 10 minutes.

**What we learned:** Seeing further helped the DQN here, but the bigger effects came from the reward and the training horizon. Judge any change against the spread across seeds, and check how the games ended before deciding why a score moved.

**Try next:**
1. **Penalise starving to stop GA looping** (reward: + starve 10). The GA's seed-0 test games ended by starving in 34 of 100 cases, and its steps per apple were 48. Starvation is currently unpunished, so looping costs nothing. _Prediction:_ GA starved games fall from 34 to under 5 out of 100, steps per game drop below 600, and the test mean rises above 32. An earlier run with the same penalty reached 33.95. DQN should barely move (within about 1.4).
2. **Raise DQN gamma to 0.99** (dqn gamma 0.95 → 0.99). DQN crashes into walls and its own body with a 20-move planning horizon at gamma 0.95. Looking further ahead in time may help it avoid traps. This is a different kind of looking further from the lesson's spatial question. _Prediction:_ DQN test mean rises from 26.6 to about 28-30, and its games get longer than 237 steps. The GA is unchanged.
3. **Test rays depth 1 as a control** (vision rays:depth=4 → rays:depth=1). Depth 4 and depth 9 scored about the same, so the real question is whether depth 4 beats depth 1 under this same reward. The short-vision baseline used a different encoding, so this run provides a cleaner control. _Prediction:_ DQN drops by about 2-5 points from 26.6 to about 22-25, and GA also falls. If depth 1 scores close to depth 4, seeing further is not what helped.

## What we learned

- **DQN gains from rays:** 26.6 test apples with rays 4 (25.1–27.9) against 20.7 for the short baseline, ranges far
  apart. Looking further than one square lets it avoid some body traps.
- **Rays 9 adds nothing over rays 4 for DQN** (26.8, 25.6–27.4). The useful information is close to the head.
- **The GA gets worse:** 32.6 (28.6–36.1) with rays 4 and 29.3 (23.9–32.1) with rays 9, against 46.7 with 11 hints. It has
  28 inputs to tune instead of 11, the rays cost time so it plays half as many steps (90 and 69 million against 185),
  and looping comes back: 16% and 14% of its test games end by starving.
- **Endings shift:** DQN's wall crashes rise from about 18% to 50% of test games (rays 4). It fails differently, not
  just less often; use Inspect on a game that ends at a wall to see what its rays showed.
- **Lesson:** more information helps a learner that can use it (DQN's gradients) and hurts one that has to find every
  weight by trial (the GA), at least within 10 minutes.
