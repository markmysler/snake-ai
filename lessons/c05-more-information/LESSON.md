## Background

Every extra input is another set of weights to learn. The GA has to find good values for all of them by mutation and selection; DQN gets a gradient for each, but an MLP still has to discover that neighbouring squares are related.

**What to watch:** Steps per second (training is slower), and how far each learning curve gets.

**Recipes**

- **whole board**: vision full, reward score, Genetic algorithm + Deep Q-learning, 10 min × 3 seeds 0–2. Changes from “rays 4” of lesson 04: vision rays:depth=4 → full.

## AI analysis

### Run `2026-10-20_131708` · whole board

**Whole board: DQN scores 30.97 on the test (3-seed mean 30.68, spread 0.32), but the GA stays near 1 (seed 0: 1.01, all 100 test games starved), and the reward, not the 1,083 inputs alone, is the likely cause.**

**DQN** (double DQN, a learner that predicts the value of each move from remembered games) did well. Its test mean was 30.97 apples per game (median 32, max 44), with 269 steps per game and only 9 steps per apple, so it moves efficiently. Its 65 body crashes and 35 wall crashes show it dies by hitting itself or the walls, not by wandering. Across seeds it was very consistent (30.34 to 30.97). Its training curve rose from 0.16 (start) to 13.83 by minute 2.7 and 19.4 by minute 3.6, then plateaued between about 19 and 23 for the rest of the 10 minutes. The training mean (22.5) sits below the test score because training still includes random exploration moves.

**GA** (evolution: a population of networks is scored, the best are kept and mutated) barely learned. Its seed-0 test mean was 1.01 (median 1, max 5), with 425 steps per game and 421 steps per apple. Every one of its 100 test games ended by starving, which means the snake looped without eating until the game forced it to stop. Across the three seeds the GA mean was 1.76 (values 1.01, 1.02, 3.25; spread 1.29), so the GA result is poor and noisy. Its population mean rose from 0.06 to about 0.58, but its curve was flat at 0.4 to 0.7 for most of the run. The GA ran about 39 million environment steps (game moves) in 10 minutes, roughly 65,000 steps per second, versus about 360,000 steps for DQN at roughly 600 per second.

**Why:** The reward is the main suspect for the GA. The score reward gives +10 per apple and -10 per crash, and nothing for starving. A snake that loops forever therefore scores 0, while one that crashes scores -10. Evolution finds that safe looping beats risky play, which matches the 100 of 100 starved test games. Runs in this lab that included a starvation penalty (for example the rays runs with starve 10) ended with few or no starved games. So this run cannot show that full vision hurts the GA: the reward gives the GA a loophole, and vision and reward changed together compared with lesson 04's rays run. The earlier 10-minute rays run without the survival term (same starve penalty, reward otherwise different) gave the GA 33.95 and DQN 26.75.

For DQN, the whole board looks helpful rather than harmful, even though the input vector grew 39 times. DQN learns to ignore most of the 1,083 inputs: its curve climbs fast once exploration falls (epsilon-greedy exploration reaches its final value around 100,000 steps, about minute 3), and then stays flat. Its plateau is more likely a learning limit within 10 minutes than a sign that more information is useless. Compute matters too: the full-vision DQN got only about 363,000 steps in 10 minutes, fewer than the rays run's roughly 920,000, yet it scored higher. Reward and step count differ between these runs, so treat that gap as suggestive, not proof.

**What we learned:** More information is not automatically better: a huge input can slow an agent (the GA here ran far fewer useful games), but a reward that accidentally rewards looping can wreck a run no matter what the snake sees. When two runs differ, check every setting, including reward and time, before blaming the one you meant to change.

## What we learned

- **The GA collapses:** 1.8 test apples (1.0–3.2), and 98% of its test games end by starving. With 1,083 inputs it has
  over 17,000 weights to evolve; random mutations almost never improve a network that size, and a snake that circles
  safely beats one that dies early.
- **DQN gets its best score of the course so far:** 30.7 (30.3–31.0) against 26.6 with rays 4, with very little spread. It
  did so with only about 0.36 million env steps, a quarter of what rays 4 got, because each step costs more.
- **So "is more information better?" depends on the learner:** for gradient descent the whole picture is worth its cost;
  for evolution in 10 minutes it is fatal. Lesson 02 of the notebook found the same with a single seed.
- Use Inspect on the DQN board: the three pictures are exactly what its network sees.
