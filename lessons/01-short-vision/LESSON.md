# 01 · Short vision (the baseline)

**Question:** With a tiny, hand-made view of the game, how well can each method learn Snake in 10 minutes?

**Change:** none, this is the starting point every later lesson is compared against.

## The setup

Two very different ways of turning experience into a policy (state in, action out):

- **Genetic algorithm (GA).** No gradients. 200 random networks each play 3 games and get a fitness
  score (`apples x 100 + steps survived x 0.1`). The best 10% survive unchanged; the rest of the next
  generation are children of good parents (mix their weights, then add random noise). Repeat.
  Learning = selection. See `train_ga.py`.
- **DQN.** One network learns to predict how much future reward each action leads to (its "Q-value"),
  from transitions stored in a replay buffer, and plays the action with the highest prediction
  (plus random moves while exploring). Learning = gradient descent on prediction error. See `train_dqn.py`.
  Two variants differ only in reward: *score* (+10 apple, -10 death) and *shaped* (also +/-0.1 for
  moving toward/away from the apple).

What every snake sees, the **short vision**, is 11 numbers (`vision.py`):
danger straight/left/right (only the next cell), current heading, and whether the apple is
left/right/up/down of the head.

## What we learned

- **In wall-clock time the GA won clearly:** 51.6 apples on average vs about 21.5 for both DQNs, and one
  GA game filled the whole board (97 apples = every free cell).
- **But it was not a fair fight on experience:** the GA used 214 million game steps, each DQN about
  0.7 million, roughly **300x less**. The GA also had 11 CPU cores; each DQN had one. Switch the dashboard
  x-axis to *env steps* (with *log x*) and the DQNs are far ahead at every amount of experience.
  Which axis is "right" depends on the question: sample efficiency (steps) or practical speed (wall-clock).
- **The DQNs plateaued** at a training mean of ~16 after about 3 minutes and barely moved after that,
  while the GA kept improving. We don't know why yet. Candidate causes, each one a possible lesson:
  the 11 features can't show traps further away than one cell; gamma 0.95 only "looks ahead" ~20 moves;
  the network is updated from a small, recent slice of experience.
- **Reward shaping made no real difference** (21.7 vs 21.3). The state already tells the snake where
  the apple is, so the extra +/-0.1 hint adds little.
- **The fitness function shapes behaviour.** GA snakes take ~27 moves per apple, DQN snakes ~8.
  The GA is paid 0.1 per step survived, so wandering is rewarded. There is also no explicit penalty
  for dying or for starving (looping until the step limit). What you reward is what you get.
- **Training scores are not test scores.** "Mean (last 100)" mixes in exploration and, for the GA,
  deliberately mutated bad genomes. The evaluation (best model, 100 fixed games, no randomness) is the
  number to compare across lessons.
