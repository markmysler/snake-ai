# 11 · jev: playing without practice

**Question:** The GA and DQN need thousands of games to learn Snake. How well does a general decision model play it
with no practice at all, told only what the snake senses each move?

**Change:** A different kind of player: jev (TypeSafe's decision model, through TokenRouter) instead of a trained
network. Short vision (the 11 hints of course lesson 01, said in words relative to the heading), instructions
"Rules + stay safe", 5 of the fixed test games.

**Prediction:** _What do you expect, and why? Write it down before looking at the results._ Compare with course lesson
01's baseline: GA 46.7, DQN 20.7 test apples with the same vision.

## What we learned

- **With no practice at all, jev plays as well as DQN after 10 minutes of training:** 26.6 test apples on its 5 games
  (23–30). On the same 5 test games, course lesson 01's DQN seeds scored 23.6, 24.0 and 25.8; its GA seeds 45.2, 39.2 and
  59.2. Five games is a small test, so read "about as good as DQN, well below the GA".
- **It plays efficiently:** about 10 moves per apple, like DQN (8–9) and much better than the GA (about 25), which
  wanders more.
- **It fails like the trained snakes:** all 5 games ended with the snake hitting itself, around 25–30 apples. The short
  vision only warns one square ahead, so no player, trained or not, sees the trap coming.
- **It knows when it is in trouble:** it gave 90% or more to its chosen move on 69% of its moves, but on all 5 fatal
  moves it was unsure (42–69%). Step to the last moves in Inspect: the snake is boxed in and every option is bad.
- **How it is said matters:** told the apple's direction on the screen (as the network gets it), jev wandered without
  eating; told it relative to its heading, it went straight for the apples. Same information, different wording: this is
  what "training" means for a prompted model.
- **Cost and speed:** 1,356 moves, 1.6 cents, 20 minutes (1 to 30 seconds per request; the decisions API is in alpha).
