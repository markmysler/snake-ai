**Question:** The short-vision snake dies by trapping itself. What if it is told how much of the board each move keeps reachable?

**Change:** Vision: the 11 short hints plus 3 numbers, the share of free squares still reachable after moving straight, left or right.

## Background

The room numbers come from a flood fill, a computation the network could hardly learn by itself from local hints. Hand-made features like this are how a small model gets knowledge it can't discover.

**What to watch:** Use Inspect: the shaded pockets show what each move leaves. Then compare body crashes with lesson 01.

**Recipes**

- **room**: vision room, reward score, Genetic algorithm + Deep Q-learning, 10 min × 3 seeds 0–2. Changes from “baseline” of lesson 01: vision short → room.

### Run `2026-10-20_131708` · room

**The room vision lifts DQN's test score from 21.3 (baseline, same reward) to 38.4 for seed 0, and to 38.6 ± 0.9 across three seeds, a clear gain; the GA's 32.1 (37.9 ± 9.5 across seeds) cannot be compared cleanly because its reward changed too.**

**DQN** (test mean 38.41 for seed 0; 38.63 ± 0.92 across seeds 0–2) gained about 17 points over the lesson 01 baseline's dqn_score agent (21.31), which used the same reward. The seed spread is under 1 point, so this gain is far beyond seed noise. Its training curve reached about 21 by 0.93 min and then stayed flat at 20–23 until the 10-minute mark, while the greedy test score stayed around 38. Steps per game rose from 177 to 423 and steps per apple from 8 to 11, so the snake lives longer but wanders more per apple.

**GA** (test mean 32.11 for seed 0; 37.89 ± 9.52 across seeds, with values 32.11, 32.69, 48.88) changed both vision and reward: the baseline GA used the ga_classic reward (apple 100, survival 0.1), and this run uses apple 10 and death 10 for every agent. Its curve sat near 0.1–0.25 until about generation 52 (1.5 min), rose to 18 by 3.4 min and about 35 by 7 min, and ended at 38.8 with its population mean still climbing (27.3). It was not converged when the time ran out. It completed about 50 million environment steps in 10 minutes (roughly 83k steps per second), against about 1.4 million for DQN (roughly 2.4k steps per second). A score of about 38 cost the GA about 35 times more environment steps than DQN, so DQN was far more sample-efficient, while the GA was far faster per second.

Ending types for seed 0: GA had 36 wall and 64 body crashes; DQN had 8 wall and 92 body crashes. Across the three seeds, body crashes averaged about 82% of GA games and about 86% of DQN games, against 80% for the baseline DQN. Starvation and wins were negligible.

**Why:** The room feature gives DQN the one thing the short vision lacks: whether a move leaves enough open space. That explains the jump from 21 to 38 and the longer games. But the lesson's premise was that the snake dies by trapping itself, and the run does not show that the feature reduced trapping. Body crashes stayed dominant, and their share rose slightly (80% to about 86%). Room tells the network how much space is left, but the reward (death 10 for any crash) does not penalise a body crash more than a wall crash, so the network has little reason to avoid trapping itself beyond what the room number already tells it.

The gap between DQN's training mean (about 21) and its greedy test score (about 38) is mostly exploration. Epsilon-greedy moves are random, and the training mean includes them, so the training curve understates the learned policy. The GA's curve is different: its population mean is dragged down by mutated children, and its elites are the networks that the test score reflects. The GA's seed spread (32 to 49) is large for one 10-minute run, so its difference from DQN is not reliable on this evidence.

**What we learned:** A hand-made feature that encodes a computation the network cannot easily learn (here, reachable space) can raise the score a lot. Check the ending types too: the feature lengthened the games, but it did not reduce the body crashes that it was meant to prevent.

## What we learned

- **DQN almost doubles:** 38.6 test apples (37.9–39.6) against 20.7 with the 11 hints alone, the biggest gain in the course,
  from 3 extra numbers. Its ranges are nowhere near each other: a real difference.
- **The GA is less clear:** 37.9 on average (32.1–48.9) against 46.7 (42.8–50.7). The ranges overlap, so this could be luck.
  The flood fill is slow, so it played about 49 million env steps instead of 185 million. One GA seed filled the board in a
  test game (97 apples).
- **What the numbers mean:** the room shares encode something a small network could hardly work out from local hints:
  whether a move walks into a pocket. Hand-made features like this are how a small model gets knowledge it can't learn.
- **But the snakes still die by hitting themselves** (82–86% of test games). Knowing the room left after one move is not
  planning; long-term traps remain.
