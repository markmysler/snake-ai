**Question:** What happens when the reward also pays the snake a little for every move it survives?

**Change:** Reward: add a survival bonus of 0.1 per move to the baseline's apple +10, death −10.

**Your prediction:** Will the snakes eat more, or find a way to collect the bonus without eating? Look at how the test games end. _Write yours down before you look at the results._

## Background

A reward is the only thing a learner optimises. Surviving 100 moves is now worth as much as an apple, and a snake that circles safely never dies. It only starves when it goes 100 × its length moves without eating, and starving costs nothing here.

**What to watch:** How the test games ended (wall, body, starved) and the steps per apple. Compare with lesson 01's baseline.

**Recipes**

- **survival bonus**: vision short, reward apple +10, death -10, survival +0.1, Genetic algorithm + Deep Q-learning, 10 min × 3 seeds 0–2. Changes from “baseline” of lesson 01: reward: + survival 0.1.

## AI analysis

### Run `2026-10-20_131708` · survival bonus

**The GA reached a test mean of 45.6 (seed group 44.3 ± 1.8) against DQN's 20.5 (20.7 ± 1.1), and the survival bonus left DQN unchanged while one GA seed learned to loop, with 25 of 100 test games ending by starvation.**

**GA (genetic algorithm, which evolves networks without gradients):** Seed 0 scored a test mean of 45.58 (median 46, max 86). Its 10 wall, 89 body and 1 starved endings are mostly honest crashes, at 27 steps per apple. The seed group averages 44.28, with seeds 1 and 2 at 45.04 and 42.22. Seed 1 is the warning sign: 25 of its 100 test games ended by starvation, at 44 steps per apple, so it wandered. Training curves rose from 0.09 to 50.5 mean over the last 100 games, and the population mean climbed from 0.08 to 45.5. The curve flattened after about 5 minutes, so the 10-minute budget was close to saturated. The GA ran about 264 million env steps, about 480k steps per second on 5 cores.

**DQN (a gradient-based learner with a replay buffer, which predicts the value of each move):** Test mean 20.54 (seed group 20.72). It ate efficiently, at 8 steps per apple, and its games ended by body (80) or wall (20), never by starvation. Its training mean of 14.9 includes exploration moves, so the test score is the figure to trust. It ran about 2.0 million env steps on one core, about 3.5k steps per second. Those are about 130 times fewer steps than the GA, yet it still learned a sensible policy. Per env step it is far more efficient, while per wall-clock minute the GA is much faster.

**Why:** The survival bonus is +0.1 per move, against +10 per apple, so one apple equals 100 surviving moves. A snake that circles can collect the bonus without eating. Starvation only ends a game after 100 times its length in moves, and it carries no penalty here (starve is not in this recipe). At length 10 that is 1000 moves, worth 100 reward, the same as ten apples. That is why looping is attractive. For the GA, which selects on total reward, looping paid in seed 1 and the population drifted toward it. Seed 0 barely did (1 starved game), and seed 2 died against walls instead (48 wall endings), so the loophole appears only in some seeds. For DQN, gamma 0.95 looks only about 20 moves ahead, and 0.1 per move is worth about 2 in discounted value. That is too small to drive looping, so DQN behaves the same as in the baseline (20.5 against 21.3).

Comparison caveat: the baseline GA used apple 100 and survival 0.1, with no death penalty. The change in this lesson was therefore larger than the lesson text suggests. Relative to apples, survival is now ten times stronger, and a death penalty was added. The baseline GA scored 51.55 on one seed. The seed group mean is 44.3 (spread ±1.8). That gap of about 7 points is larger than the seed spread, but the baseline has only one seed, so treat it as suggestive rather than settled. No prediction was recorded in the run, so the lesson's written prediction cannot be checked here.

**What we learned:** A reward is optimised literally: a bonus that pays for surviving can be earned by looping without eating, and whether an agent finds that loophole depends on the seed. Always read how the test games ended, not only the score.

**Try next:**
1. **Penalise starving to stop looping** (reward: + starve 30). Seed 1 looped (25 of 100 test games starved, 44 steps per apple), while seed 0 barely did. If a starvation penalty removes the loophole, looping should disappear across seeds without hurting the honest GA. _Prediction:_ GA starved games fall from 25 (seed 1) and 1 (seed 0) to under 3 per 100 games; GA steps per apple fall toward 25-30; GA test mean stays within 42-47.
2. **Replace survival bonus with hunger cost** (reward: + hunger 0.01; reward: − survival). A per-move cost makes waiting bad, so the loophole closes without a separate starvation rule. Hunger and survival are mutually exclusive, so this is a clean swap. _Prediction:_ GA starved games drop to 0-2 per 100; GA steps per apple fall below 30; GA test mean rises to about 46-50, since eating sooner is rewarded.

## What we learned

- **DQN ignored the bonus:** 20.7 test apples (19.7–21.9), the same as the baseline (20.4–21.2), and it never starved. At
  0.1 per move, surviving is worth far less to it than the apples it can reach.
- **The GA took it, but not every seed:** 44.3 (42.2–45.6) against 46.7 for the baseline, a small drop within the
  seeds' overlap. One seed learned to loop and starved in 25 of its 100 test games; the others in 0 and 1. On average it
  now needs 33 moves per apple instead of 26.
- **Why the GA:** its fitness is the total reward of a whole game, so a network that circles safely for hundreds of moves
  collects a bonus no apple-chaser can beat without risk. Selection finds that loophole in some runs and not others.
- **Lesson:** a reward is a specification, and learners exploit its gaps. One seed would have told you either "no
  effect" or "it ruins the GA" depending on luck. Try *Make starving cost something* next.
