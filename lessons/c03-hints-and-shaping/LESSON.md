**Question:** Does a small hint for moving toward the apple help DQN learn faster, and can a hint mislead it?

**Change:** DQN only. Reward: no hint, a distance hint (+0.1 closer, −0.1 further), or a potential-based hint of the same size.

**Your prediction:** Which hint helps most early on? Does any of them change the final test score? _Write yours down before you look at the results._

## Background

Reward shaping adds small rewards for progress. A plain distance hint can change which strategy is best: every step away from the apple now costs something, so safe detours look bad. The potential-based version is built so that it provably can't change the best strategy; it only points the way.

**What to watch:** The first minutes of the learning curves (switch to Env steps), then the test scores and their spread across seeds.

**Recipes**

- **no hint**: vision short, reward score, Deep Q-learning, 10 min × 3 seeds 0–2.
- **distance hint**: vision short, reward shaped, Deep Q-learning, 10 min × 3 seeds 0–2. Changes from “no hint”: reward: + distance 0.1.
- **potential hint**: vision short, reward apple +10, death -10, potential +0.1, Deep Q-learning, 10 min × 3 seeds 0–2. Changes from “no hint”: reward: + potential 0.1.

**Terms** (explained in the dashboard's Learn tab): Distance hint, Potential-based hint, Reward shaping, Deep Q-learning (DQN), Seed spread.

## AI analysis

### Run `2026-10-20_131708-6` · distance hint

**The distance hint gave DQN no measurable gain: this seed scored 20.82 on the fixed test (the three seeds average 21.22 ± 0.43), in the same range as the no-hint runs (20.44 to 21.31).**

DQN with the distance hint (+0.1 for each step closer to the apple, −0.1 for each step away) reached a test mean of 20.82 apples on seed 2, with median 21 and best game 41. Across the three hint seeds the test means were 21.68, 21.16 and 20.82 (mean 21.22, std 0.43). The no-hint runs in the record scored 21.31 (baseline, seed 0), 20.44 (seed 1) and 21.20 (seed 2). The gap between the two groups is about 0.2 points, smaller than the 0.43 spread across seeds, so this run gives no evidence that the hint helps or hurts the final score.

How the snake fails: 83 of 100 test games ended by hitting its own body and 17 by hitting a wall, with none starving. It averaged 171 steps per game and 8 steps per apple, so it moves efficiently toward food and then traps itself as the body grows. The training curve rose fast, from a mean of 0.23 in the first 0.02 minutes to 15.57 by about 0.9 minutes, then flattened between 14 and 16.5 for the rest of the 10 minutes (final mean of the last 100 games: 14.41). The best single game kept improving slowly (37 to 46), while the average did not. Throughput fell from about 6,700 to about 4,000 env steps per second as training went on. The run used 2.6 million env steps; the no-hint runs used 2.0 and 2.5 million, so speed varies with the shared machine as well as the algorithm.

**Why:** The hint is small (0.1 per step, against 10 per apple), so it nudges the snake without changing what a good strategy is. That explains why the score barely moves. The bottleneck is what the snake can see: the short vision only shows the square directly ahead, so it cannot tell that its body is curling around into a dead end. That is why body collisions dominate (83%) and the hint cannot fix them. The training mean also reflects exploration only early on: with eps_decay_steps at 100000, exploration is nearly finished after the first minute or so, so the later plateau is mostly about the learned policy. Because this run has no no-hint curve in the record, it cannot say whether the hint speeds early learning; the curve alone does not show that.

**What we learned:** A small reward hint does not change the final score when the bottleneck is what the agent can see; check the crash type (here, body collisions at 83%) before tuning rewards. Judge any effect against the seed spread, not against one run.

**Try next:**
1. **Let the snake see further with rays** (vision short → rays:depth=4). Body collisions are 83% of crashes, and the short vision is blind beyond one square, so the snake cannot see its own tail coming. Rays look up to 4 squares along 8 lines, which should reveal dead ends earlier. _Prediction:_ DQN test mean rises to about 25 to 30 apples, and the body-collision share falls below 70% of test games.
2. **Make the distance hint ten times louder** (reward: distance 0.1 → 1). This tests whether the hint is too weak to matter or whether a stronger hint would pull the snake off course. The lesson's Try this asks the same question. _Prediction:_ DQN test mean falls below 19 apples, with more wall or body crashes as the snake chases the hint instead of the apple.
3. **Swap distance hint for potential hint** (reward: − distance; reward: + potential 0.1). The potential-based hint is designed not to change the best strategy, so it is a useful control against the distance hint at the same size. _Prediction:_ Test mean within about ±0.5 of this run's 20.82, with no clear change in early learning.

### Run `2026-10-20_131708-9` · potential hint

**The potential hint scored 21.84 on this seed's 100 test games, but across its three seeds the score is 21.38 ± 0.61, which is the same as the no-hint and distance-hint runs.**

DQN (the Q-learning agent that learns the value of each move from replayed experience) with the potential hint averaged 21.84 apples on the test games (median 21, max 41). Its three seeds gave 21.61, 20.69 and 21.84. The no-hint runs (seeds 1 and 2) gave 20.44 and 21.20, and the distance hint gave 21.68, 21.16 and 20.82. Every spread overlaps, so the hint made no detectable difference to the final score.

The test games show the snake plays cleanly: 181 steps per game and 8 steps per apple, with no starvation. Of the 100 games, 85 ended by crashing into its own body and 15 into a wall. The training curve climbs quickly. At the first logged point after the start (0.92 min, 259k env steps) the mean of the last 100 games was already 14.12, and it then wandered between 14 and 17 until the 10-minute mark (16.33). The first minute did the learning, and the remaining nine minutes added almost nothing.

**Why:** The short vision shows only the adjacent square in each direction. Once the snake is long, it cannot see its own tail until it is already next to it, so body collisions dominate (85 of 100). That limit comes from the input, not from the reward, which is why changing the hint barely moved the score. A potential-based hint (a reward for moving toward the apple that provably leaves the best strategy unchanged) mainly speeds early learning, and this run had already learned most of what the short view allows by its first logged point. The hint's early effect cannot be read from these curves, because the logs are about a minute apart. Also, this run shared the machine with another run, which affects speed (about 4,200 steps per second) but not the score.

**What we learned:** A reward hint can speed learning only when the agent's view can use the hint. Compare hints using the spread across seeds and the test score, not the single training curve, and check whether the agent's inputs limit it more than its reward does.

## What we learned

- **Neither hint made a difference you can trust:** no hint 20.7 (20.4–21.2), distance hint 21.2 (20.8–21.7), potential
  hint 21.4 (20.7–21.8). All three ranges overlap.
- **Why:** the short vision already tells the snake which way the apple is, so a hint for moving toward it adds almost no
  information. Shaping helps most when the goal is hard to find; here the limit is seeing traps, and about 80% of test
  games still end with the snake hitting itself.
- **No harm either:** the distance hint could in principle change the best strategy, but at 0.1 per move it didn't.
  Try *A hint that is too loud* to see it go wrong.
- With one seed per recipe, a 0.7-apple gain could easily have been reported as "potential shaping helps".
