# 02 · Full map vision

**Question:** If the snake can see the whole board instead of 11 hand-picked hints, does it play better?

**Change:** only what the networks see (`--vision full`, see `vision.py`). Every other setting is the
same as [lesson 01](../01-short-vision/LESSON.md): same networks' hidden sizes, same rewards, same
GA and DQN settings, same 10-minute budget, same machine.

## What "full vision" means

The short vision from lesson 01 answers three questions about the cells right next to the head
("will I die if I go straight / left / right?") plus "which way am I heading?" and "roughly where
is the food?". It cannot see a wall of its own body two cells away, which is why those snakes
eventually coil into a dead end.

Full vision gives the network the whole board as three stacked pictures:

| picture | value in each cell |
|---|---|
| blocked | 1 for walls and for body cells that will still be there next move |
| body | how many more moves each body cell stays occupied (÷ length), so the network can tell the tail end frees up soon |
| food | 1 where the apple is |

Two tricks make those pictures much easier to learn from:

1. **Centred on the head.** The picture is 19×19 for a 10×10 board, so the whole board is always
   visible wherever the head is, and "the cell in front of me" is always the same input number.
2. **Rotated so the snake faces up.** The actions are relative (turn left / straight / turn right),
   so after rotating, "left" always means "the left side of the picture". Without this the network
   would have to learn the same rule four times, once per heading.

That is 3 × 19 × 19 = **1,083 inputs instead of 11**.

## Prediction (written before the run)

- Seeing more *should* let the snakes avoid trapping themselves, so the eventual ceiling should be higher.
- But everything gets bigger: the GA's network goes from 243 to 17,395 weights for evolution to search,
  and a DQN training step costs ~15x more compute. Building the picture also makes the game itself ~12x slower.
- So within the same 10 minutes we expect **fewer steps, slower learning, and lower scores than lesson 01**,
  especially for the GA. Compare the runs on the *env steps* x-axis to separate "learns less per step"
  from "got fewer steps".

## What we learned

- **Same 10 minutes, lower scores, as predicted.** Evaluation (mean apples over 100 fixed games):
  GA 3.0 (lesson 01: 51.6), DQN score 18.6 (21.3), DQN shaped 17.2 (21.7).
- **For DQN, the loss is all compute, not learning.** In 10 minutes each DQN got only ~91k game steps
  (lesson 01: ~725k). Compare at the *same* number of steps (dashboard: x-axis = Env steps) and the two
  visions are level: at ~91k steps lesson 01's DQNs had a training mean of 7.1 / 6.3, lesson 02's have
  6.7 / 7.2. Short vision just reached that point after 2 minutes instead of 10.
  Our prediction of "slower learning" was **wrong** for DQN: it learns just as fast per step, each step is just ~5x more expensive.
- **For the GA, full vision really does learn worse.** At the same ~30M steps, lesson 01's GA already
  averaged 18.5 apples (best 65); lesson 02's averages 1 (best 15). Evolution now has to search 17,395
  weights instead of 243, and its settings were tuned for the small network: each child gets ~1,700
  random weight changes, and the initial weights are so large for 1,083 inputs that the hidden layer
  starts out saturated (every neuron stuck near +1 or -1).
- **More information is not automatically better.** It only helps if the learner can afford to use it.
  A bigger input means a bigger model, slower games and slower updates, and an optimizer (here the GA's
  mutation settings) that may need re-tuning.
- **Always look at both x-axes.** Wall-clock alone would say "full vision is worse". Env steps shows
  DQN is equally sample-efficient with it, while the GA is genuinely worse.

**Ideas for next lessons** (change one thing at a time):
- Give full vision equal *experience* instead of equal time (e.g. a longer budget) to see whether it
  eventually passes short vision, which is the original hope: seeing traps coming.
- Make each DQN step cheaper: batch size 64 instead of 256, more torch threads, or a small convolutional
  network that reuses the same "look for walls" filter everywhere on the picture.
- Fix the GA for big networks: start from smaller weights (scaled by 1/sqrt(inputs)) and mutate less.
- A middle ground: a small window around the head (e.g. 7x7) instead of the whole board.
