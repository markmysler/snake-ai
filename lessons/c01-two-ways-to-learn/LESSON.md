## Background

The GA never computes a gradient: 200 networks each play 3 games, the best 10% are kept and the rest are replaced by mutated children of good parents. DQN trains one network to predict the future reward of each move from stored experience, and plays the move it rates highest (plus random moves while exploring). Both see the same 11 numbers: danger next to the head, the heading, and which way the apple is.

**What to watch:** Switch the learning curve to Env steps with log x: the DQN line is far ahead at every amount of experience. Then look at how the test games ended.

**Recipes**

- **baseline**: vision short, reward score, Genetic algorithm + Deep Q-learning, 10 min × 3 seeds 0–2.

## AI analysis

### Run `2026-10-20_131820` · baseline

**The GA won clearly on the test games (50.72 vs 20.36 for DQN in this seed; across 3 seeds 46.7 ± 4.0 vs 20.7 ± 0.5), but DQN reached its score with about 1.8M env steps against the GA's 232M.**

**GA (evolution):** The test mean is 50.72 (median 53.5, best game 87). Each test game lasts about 1,275 steps and about 25 steps per apple. 90 of 100 games ended by hitting its own body and 10 by hitting a wall. Its training curve climbed steadily from 0.3 at about 0.5 minutes to 51.2 at 10 minutes and had not levelled off. The population mean of 40.7 at the end shows that most children were still improving. Across the 3 seeds the GA scored 42.75 to 50.72, so this seed sits at the top of the range.

**DQN (Q-learning):** The test mean is 20.36 (median 21, best game 40). Games last about 168 steps, about 8 steps per apple. 82 of 100 games ended by body collision and 18 by wall. Its curve rose to about 15.5 within the first minute and then stayed flat at 15 to 17 for the remaining nine minutes, while it ran about 3,900 steps per second against the GA's roughly 400,000. The three DQN seeds agree closely (20.36 to 21.2).

The lesson's note that the DQN line is 'far ahead at every amount of experience' holds only for the first 2 to 3 million env steps. After that the GA's training mean passes DQN's, and by 33M steps it is at 29, more than DQN's final 17 after its whole run.

**Why:** Both snakes die by crashing, not starving, so the main limit is that the 'short' vision only shows the one square ahead. The snake cannot see that its own body is closing off the space around it, so it walks into a trap. The GA partly makes up for this by evolving long survival and eating about 50 apples per game, and it has 232M steps to try variants. DQN has 1.8M steps, and its one-square view plus a single learned value function seems to cap it near 15 to 20 apples. Its flat curve after one minute suggests it has learned what the short view can express, not that it is still exploring: epsilon (the random-move rate) decays over the first 100k steps, which is about the first minute of this run.

The score reward (+10 per apple, -10 for death) is the same for both. It gives no help in avoiding self-traps, and the GA is paying for each apple with far more trial games.

**What we learned:** Sample efficiency (score per env step) and speed (steps per second) are different measures, and the winner depends on which one you care about. DQN learns quickly from few steps but then plateaus, while the GA is slow per step but keeps climbing with enough steps.

**Try next:**
1. **Give the snake a room-aware vision** (vision short → room). Most test games end with the snake running into its own body (82% DQN, 90% GA). The room vision adds a flood fill that shows how much free space each move leaves, which is the information needed to avoid boxing itself in. If the crash mix changes, the cause was blindness to the trap. _Prediction:_ DQN's body-collision share falls below 70% of test games (from 82%), and its test mean rises by 2 or more points above 20.4. The GA improves less.
2. **Try a bigger 15 by 15 board** (board 10x10 → 15x15). On a bigger board apples are farther away and games last longer. This tests whether the GA's lead holds when the game gets harder, which is the lesson's own first Try this task. _Prediction:_ The GA stays above 35 on the test games, and its steps per apple rise above 25. DQN stays below 25.
3. **Look six squares ahead with rays** (vision short → rays:depth=6). The short vision sees only one square, so the snake cannot notice its body coming up on the far side. Rays look up to N squares along eight lines, so a depth of 6 tests whether seeing farther than one square is what limits DQN. _Prediction:_ DQN's test mean rises to about 23 to 27, and its body-collision share falls below 75%. The GA changes by less than DQN.

## What we learned

- **In wall-clock time the GA wins clearly:** 46.7 test apples (seeds 42.8–50.7) against 20.7 for DQN (20.4–21.2). Its best
  test game reached 90 apples.
- **On experience it is the other way round:** the GA played about 185 million env steps, DQN about 1.45 million, roughly
  **130× less**. Switch the chart to *Env steps* with *log x*: at every amount of experience DQN is far ahead. DQN is the
  sample-efficient learner, the GA the fast one.
- **Both die the same way:** about 80% of test games end with the snake hitting itself. With 11 hints it can't see a trap
  until it is one square away, so it fills in its own escape routes as it grows.
- **DQN levels off** (its training mean ends around 16) while the GA keeps improving. Its training mean is
  below its test score because training includes random exploration moves; the GA's curve (its kept networks, 48.8)
  is close to its test score.
- **Seeds matter more for the GA:** its three seeds span 8 apples, DQN's less than 1.
