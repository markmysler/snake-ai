## Background

A reward k moves away counts gamma^k: at 0.95 a crash 20 moves ahead is worth a third of an immediate one; at 0.99 it is still worth 80%. Longer horizons see more, but their targets are noisier and take longer to learn.

**What to watch:** The learning curves over env steps, and the share of body crashes in the test games.

**Recipes**

- **gamma 0.99**: vision short, reward score, Deep Q-learning, 10 min × 3 seeds 0–2. Changes from “no hint” of lesson 03: dqn gamma 0.95 → 0.99.

### Run `2026-10-20_131708` · gamma 0.99

**Raising DQN's planning horizon from gamma 0.95 to 0.99 changed nothing measurable: the test mean is 21.4 ± 0.7 across three seeds (21.31 at 0.95), and 81% of seed 0's test games end by crashing into its own body.**

**DQN (gamma 0.99, short vision, score reward, 10 min):** Seed 0 scored a test mean of 22.02 (median 21, best 35) over 100 fixed-seed games. Across the three seeds the test mean is 21.4 (range 20.67 to 22.02, std 0.68). The earlier gamma 0.95 baseline with the same vision and reward scored 21.31 on one seed, well inside that spread. Seed 0 took 195 steps per game and 9 steps per apple. Of its games, 81 ended in a body crash, 19 in a wall crash, and none starved.

The learning curve rose quickly and then flattened. The training mean of the last 100 games was 15.57 by minute 0.92 and stayed between 13 and 16.5 for the rest of the run, ending at 15.51. The best training game (41 apples) appeared by minute 4.6 and was never beaten. Because the mean only includes exploration moves, it reads lower than the greedy test score. Seed 0 logged 1.52M env steps, while seeds 1 and 2 logged about 2.45M each. The machine was shared by two runs, so that gap reflects load, not learning. Compare the algorithms by score per env step, not by wall-clock speed, and treat differences of about one point between single runs as noise.

**Why:** The most likely reason gamma did not help is that the short vision sees only the adjacent square. A longer planning horizon can only help if the agent can perceive the danger it is planning around. A snake that cannot see its body coming from several squares away will keep curling into itself, and valuing future crashes more does not give it the information to avoid them. The 81% body-crash share fits this. The plateau from minute 1 onward suggests the limit is what the agent observes, not how long it trains.

The lesson's own prediction was left blank in this run, so there is nothing to score. The other seeds (21.52, 20.67) and the lesson 03 baseline (21.31) support the view that the discount factor is not the bottleneck here.

**What we learned:** Planning further ahead only pays off when the agent can actually see the danger it is planning around. Before tuning how DQN values the future, check which crashes it makes and whether its inputs can reveal them.

## What we learned

- **No real change:** gamma 0.99 scored 21.4 (20.7–22.0) against 20.7 (20.4–21.2) with gamma 0.95. The ranges overlap.
- **Body crashes didn't drop** (about 82% of test games either way). Valuing the future more doesn't help when the inputs
  can't show the trap coming: the snake can only see one square ahead, so it has nothing to plan with.
- **Compare with lesson 06:** 3 inputs about room to move raised DQN to 38.6. What the snake can see limits it far more
  than how far ahead it values rewards.
- Gamma 0.99 might matter with a richer vision (rays or room) or a longer training time; that would be a good exercise.
