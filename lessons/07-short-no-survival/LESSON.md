**Removing the survival reward stopped the GA from looping: starved games fell from 22 to 0 of 100 and the GA test mean rose from 37.7 to 43.5 (one seed).**

**DQN (Deep Q-learning, which learns the value of each move from stored experience):** test mean 20.86 (median 21, max 35), 170 steps per game, 8 steps per apple. Test games ended by body collision 83, wall 17. Its training curve climbed quickly to about 15 by minute 1.1 (110,776 env steps), then stayed flat around 14-16 for the rest of the run. Only 262,816 env steps were taken in 3 minutes, against 66 million for the GA. Its test score is higher than its training mean (14.58) because training still includes random exploration moves.

**Why:** The survival reward paid the GA for staying alive, so looping on the spot was a profitable strategy: 22 of 100 test games ended by starvation, with 49 steps per apple. Removing it removed that payoff. Starvation dropped to zero and steps per apple dropped to 28, both as predicted. The GA now takes more risks, so body crashes rose from 63 to 91 of 100 games. That is the trade-off: once staying alive earns nothing, the snake hits its own body sooner. The short vision only sees one square ahead, so it cannot tell that a turn will trap it. That explains the high body-crash share for both agents.

The GA gain of about 6 points rests on one seed. The spread between seeds is unknown, so treat the 37.7 to 43.5 change as a hint, not a result. DQN barely moved (19.4 to 20.9, a 1.4 point change), which fits the idea that a small per-move survival reward mattered mostly to the GA's fitness measure. Its high speed per step is typical of a gradient learner that learns from every move. The GA's advantage here comes mostly from the 11 CPU cores it was given (DQN had 1), not from better learning per step.

**What we learned:** A reward that pays for surviving can teach an agent to stall; here, removing it cut starvation from 22% to 0% and raised the score a little. Judge a change by the test score over several seeds, and compare algorithms by score per env step and per unit of compute, not by a single wall-clock run.
