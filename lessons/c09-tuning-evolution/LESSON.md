**Question:** Which GA settings matter: judging each network on more games, or mutating less?

**Change:** GA only. Games per genome 10 instead of 3, or mutation rate 0.05 instead of 0.1.

**Your prediction:** More games make fitness fairer but each generation 3× slower. Fewer mutations change less per generation. Which wins in 10 minutes? _Write yours down before you look at the results._

## Background

Fitness from 3 games is noisy: a lucky network can be kept over a better one (the winner's curse). More games per genome reduce that noise but cost time. The mutation rate sets how far children move from their parents: too high destroys what works, too low explores slowly.

**What to watch:** The GA curves: the solid line follows the networks it keeps, the dotted one the whole generation. Generations per minute drop with 10 games.

**Recipes**

- **GA baseline**: vision short, reward score, Genetic algorithm, 10 min × 3 seeds 0–2.
- **10 games per genome**: vision short, reward score, Genetic algorithm, 10 min × 3 seeds 0–2. Changes from “GA baseline”: ga games 3 → 10.
- **mutation rate 0.05**: vision short, reward score, Genetic algorithm, 10 min × 3 seeds 0–2. Changes from “GA baseline”: ga mutation_rate 0.1 → 0.05.

## AI analysis

### Run `2026-10-20_131708-6` · 10 games per genome

**Judging each GA network on 10 games instead of 3 did not measurably change the result: this seed's test mean was 46.5, and across three seeds it averaged 45.99 ± 7.7, against 48.4 and 42.8 for the two 3-game baseline seeds shown.**

The GA (genetic algorithm: a population of small networks is mutated and selected each generation, with no gradients) reached a test mean of 46.5 over 100 fixed-seed games, with median 47 and best game 88. Of those 100 test games, 95 ended by crashing into its own body, 5 into a wall, none by starving, and none by filling the board. It took 27 steps per apple, the same as the baseline. Its kept networks (the solid curve, the best networks the GA has kept so far) climbed from 0.14 to 43.6 games-mean over 10 minutes. The curve was still rising at the end (37.4 at 8.5 min, 43.6 at 10 min), so the GA had not converged. The whole-generation mean (dotted, population_mean) rose from 0.09 to 29.3, which is expected because most members are mutated children.

The run used 235 generations in 10 minutes (470,000 games at 2,000 games per generation), which is about 2.7 times fewer generations than the 3-game baseline (about 625 generations for 375,000 games). It still used about the same number of environment steps (228M, vs. 223M and 328M for the baseline seeds), because the cost per step is the same and the number of games per generation is what changed. Across the seed group the test scores were 53.4, 38.1 and 46.5. Seed 0 is the only one that filled the board (20 of 100 test games won, max 97 apples), which shows how large the seed-to-seed spread is.

**Why:** Most likely the 10-game setting makes each fitness estimate fairer (less winner's curse, where a network that got lucky on 3 games is kept over a better one), but at 10 minutes that gain is roughly cancelled by having 2.7 times fewer generations. Both effects are small compared with seed noise (std 7.7), so this single seed cannot separate them. The bigger limit looks like the input: the short vision sees only the squares next to the head, so the snake cannot tell that its own tail is a few squares away. That is why 95% of its test games end in a self-crash. A 16-unit network with 11 inputs, trained for 10 minutes, mostly learns to avoid immediate danger and to chase apples, and the body-collision share is the clearest sign of that.

**What we learned:** More games per genome make the GA's fitness more reliable but cost generations, and at 10 minutes these two effects cancel out. A single seed cannot show a difference this small, so always compare against the spread across seeds before deciding a setting helped.

**Try next:**
1. **Try rays vision with this GA** (vision short → rays:depth=4). 95% of this run's test games end by self-crash, and short vision cannot see the body beyond one square. Rays look up to 4 squares along 8 lines, so they can show the body coming. If vision is the limit, the test mean should rise and the body share should fall. _Prediction:_ Test mean rises above about 55, and the body-collision share falls below 80% (from 95%), with steps per apple near or below 27.
2. **Mutate less with 10 games** (ga mutation_rate 0.1 → 0.05). The kept networks were still improving at the 10-minute mark (37 to 44 in the last 1.5 minutes), and a lower mutation rate changes fewer weights per child, which can help a network that is already good keep improving. This is the other arm of the lesson. _Prediction:_ Test mean between 40 and 55, most likely near 46 to 50, with the spread across seeds still overlapping this run's (38 to 53). Final kept-network mean rises a little faster in the last minutes.
3. **Try a population of 500** (ga population 200 → 500). With 10 games per genome, each generation is costly, so a bigger population means fewer generations. This tests whether more variety per generation beats the loss of generations at 10 minutes. _Prediction:_ Fewer generations (about 95 instead of 235), and a test mean that stays within the seed spread (about 40 to 55) rather than a clear jump.
