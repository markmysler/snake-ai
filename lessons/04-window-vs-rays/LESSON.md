DQN (the Q-learning agent that learns the value of each move from stored experience) reached a test mean of 27.06 (median 28, best 44) over 100 fixed-seed games. It ended 69 games by hitting its body, 31 by hitting a wall and none by starving. It averaged 240 steps per game and 9 steps per apple, so it is efficient and quick to die rather than wandering. Its training curve climbed quickly: mean of the last 100 games was about 16 after 0.9 minutes (110k steps), then hovered between 19 and 21 for the remaining four minutes, ending at 20.64. That is a plateau, not a steady climb. The training mean includes exploration (random moves that DQN still takes), so the test score is the better measure.

The GA (genetic algorithm: a population of networks that are mutated and selected, with no gradients) reached a test mean of 25.05 (median 26, best 31). It ended 86 games by body collision, 11 by wall and 3 by starvation, with 559 steps per game and 22 steps per apple. Its curve rose to about 16 by 1.2 minutes and then stayed flat around 15 to 17 for the rest of the run, ending at 16.67. It ran about 66 million environment steps, against 0.47 million for DQN, because it uses 11 cores in parallel. Its test mean fell 5.4 points from the window run (30.49 to 25.05), which is the largest change in this comparison. With one seed, that could be noise, since GA results vary from one population draw to the next.

**Why:** The longer sight lines did not give DQN a clear advantage, which suggests that seeing four squares along a line is not the missing piece. Both vision types already show the nearest body part on the nearby squares, so the snake was not blind to its own body. On a 10x10 board, a snake of about 25 to 30 segments fills a large share of the space, and most deaths probably come from boxing itself in several moves ahead, which neither vision reports directly. The 5-minute budget gave DQN only 471k steps, and its curve had flattened, so a reward or time change may matter more than vision here. The GA's drop is probably seed noise, or the best-model choice for the test, because its training mean was unchanged (16.71 vs 16.67) while its test score moved by 5 points.

**What we learned:** Changing the sensor only helps if the crashes come from information the sensor lacks; here DQN still dies from its own body with almost the same frequency. Always read the test mean together with how the games ended and against the spread of a single seed.
